Do I Need a Permit to Remodel My Bathroom or Kitchen in NJ?

Which NJ bathroom and kitchen remodels need a permit, which don't, who files it and which office handles your town. Call (732) 845-6440.

By the FIXALL Remodeling teamUpdated November 9, 2026Monmouth County, NJ
  1. Home
  2. Guides
  3. Do I Need a Permit to Remodel My Bathroom or Kitchen in NJ?

Short answer: if you're only changing finishes, usually not. If you're moving plumbing, gas, wiring or walls, usually yes. Your town's construction office has the final word. This is general information, not legal advice.

What usually doesn't need a permit

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code calls some work "ordinary maintenance" (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7). In a house, that includes finish work like painting, wallpaper, tile, flooring, cabinets and countertops. There are limits, so check with your town before you assume.

What usually needs one

  • Moving or adding plumbing: a new drain, a moved toilet, a new shower valve or a gas line.
  • New electrical circuits or wiring.
  • Taking out or moving a wall, especially a bearing wall.
  • Cutting a new window or door opening.
  • Turning a basement into living space.
  • Any addition, dormer or second story.

A remodel can need more than one kind of permit: building, plumbing and electrical. Each one gets inspected.

Who files it

The plumbing and electrical parts are filed by the plumber and the electrician doing that work. On our jobs, we make sure every required permit is issued before work starts. Plumbing by our sister company, FIXALL Plumbing Heating & Air, NJ Master Plumber Lic. #36BI01212500.

Inspections

Expect inspections at the rough stage, before the walls close, and a final inspection at the end. Keep the paperwork. It matters when you sell.

Zoning comes first on bigger jobs

Additions, dormers and anything that changes the footprint or the height can need zoning approval before a construction permit. Some towns spell it out. In Little Silver, zoning approval from the borough's Planning & Zoning Office comes first, then permits through Rumson's construction office. In Manalapan, the Zoning Officer signs off first where it applies.

Historic districts

Middletown has 5 designated historic districts. In Freehold Borough's Historic Preservation District, a project goes through a Certificate of Appropriateness review with the Historic Preservation Commission. East Brunswick's Old Bridge Historic District is on the State and National Registers. If your house is in a historic district, ask about extra review before any outside work.

Condos and 55+ communities

If you live in a condo or an active-adult community, the association usually has its own approval, separate from the town's.

Which office handles your town

Two shared services to know: Matawan's permits go through Aberdeen Township's Construction Department, and Fair Haven and Little Silver go through Rumson's office.
